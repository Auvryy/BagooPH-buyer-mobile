## Contract and evidence

The existing checkout controller requires an active approved buyer and issues a signed checkout token. Its submission service accepts the documented 132-character `v1`/`v2` token shape, binds its signature and retained submissions to buyer/cart, and rejects a changed payload or stale prices. Client shape checks are usability safeguards; the server verifies signatures and replay eligibility. Quotes carry monetary/selection data rather than a new authenticated profile, so the opening session must also remain current when a quote finishes.

Initial exploration reproduced 17 failures involving stale drafts/reviews, denied or late quotes, GPS after an account change, late/foreign order replies and malformed opening identity/token data. Checkout now binds to its opening buyer and session, validates the actual signed-form shape, rejects late results and discards private drafts and retry intent after access loss. Existing same-account uncertain retries retain their exact payload and token. Fixtures now include the real token shape and authenticated order-result metadata rather than placeholder responses that the backend does not serve.

Nine additional navigation cases cover Buy Now, Bag, native links and order-result replacement, including changes before tapping, during a request and between route creation and its first build. Entry points retain their opening scope; result replacement captures a separate acknowledged scope before the old checkout route is disposed. Recorded-order/payment views also guard their actions and replies. The combined focused run passes 53 cases.

A checkpoint passes all 616 isolated cases, clean analysis and 69 native checkout/product/Bag/voucher/payment/layout cases on the private display.
